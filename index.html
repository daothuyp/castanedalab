How cells decide which proteins to keep, and which to clear.

We study shuttle proteins — proteins like UBQLN2 that escort damaged, ubiquitinated proteins to the proteasome, and that also condense into liquid droplets under stress. By comparing UBQLN2 with its relatives across the tree of life — the other human ubiquilins, yeast and Arabidopsis Dsk2, C. elegans UBQLN, and the shuttle protein RAD23 — we're after a general question: what actually governs a shuttle protein's ability to phase separate, and does that biophysical property predict whether it forms condensates in cells?

Shuttle proteins & protein quality control

UBQLN2 is one member of a family of shuttle proteins — including the other human ubiquilins and RAD23 — that recognize ubiquitinated proteins and deliver them to the proteasome for degradation.

What governs phase separation?

Comparing UBQLN2 with distant relatives — yeast and Arabidopsis Dsk2, C. elegans UBQLN — helps us ask what general principles drive a shuttle protein to phase separate, and whether that ability correlates with condensate formation in cells.

Retrotransposon-derived proteins

Domesticated retrotransposon proteins such as RTL8 bind UBQLN2 directly. We're mapping how this interaction competes with UBQLN2's proteasomal shuttling role.
