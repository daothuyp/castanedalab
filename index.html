How cells decide which proteins to keep, and which to clear.

We study ubiquilin-2 (UBQLN2), a protein quality-control factor that shuttles damaged proteins to the proteasome and also self-assembles into liquid droplets under stress. Mutations in UBQLN2 cause inherited forms of ALS and frontotemporal dementia, and we want to know why.

Protein quality control

UBQLN2 recognizes damaged and misfolded proteins and delivers them to the proteasome for degradation, a job that becomes harder to sustain as cells age.

Liquid-liquid phase separation

Under stress, UBQLN2 condenses into liquid droplets and is recruited to stress granules. We use NMR, microscopy, and scattering to track how this transition unfolds.

Retrotransposon-derived proteins

Domesticated retrotransposon proteins such as RTL8 bind UBQLN2 directly. We're mapping how this interaction competes with UBQLN2's proteasomal shuttling role.
