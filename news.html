News

Lab news

Papers, awards, and other milestones from the lab.

  • 2026

    Undergraduate researcher Mallory Brown awarded a Goldwater Scholarship

    Mallory, a double major in neuroscience and statistics, was recognized for her work on domesticated retrotransposon proteins in the lab and is currently helping determine the biomolecular structure of these proteins.

  • 2026

    Three new papers on ubiquilin structure and phase separation

    New work from the lab appeared in Advanced Science, Chemical Science, and The EMBO Journal this year, covering intramolecular interactions in ubiquilins, polyubiquitin-controlled phase separation, and how STI1 domains drive Dsk2 condensation. See the Publications page for details.

  • Jun 30, 2026

    Featured in Syracuse University Today: "A Scientist's Quest to Outsmart Brain Disease"

    Writer Rob Enslin profiled the lab's NIH-funded work on how condensates interface with protein quality control, and how that connects to ALS, FTD, and Alzheimer's. The piece also highlights undergraduate researcher Mallory Brown's work on retrotransposon proteins.

  • Ongoing

    The lab has moved to the Life Sciences Complex

    Our labs and offices are now located in Syracuse University's Life Sciences Complex. See the Contact page for details.

  • Aug 11, 2024

    Collaborative paper with the Kraut lab published in PNAS

    Using an in vitro system with ubiquitinated substrates, UBQLN2, proteasomes, and different deubiquitinases, the lab explored the functional consequences of UBQLN2 phase separation.

  • Mar 15, 2024

    New paper on ALS-linked UBQLN2 mutations, with the Sharkey, Barmada, and Paulson labs

    Published in Scientific Reports, this collaborative study examined how different ALS-linked mutations affect UBQLN2's tendency to aggregate and its neurotoxicity.

  • Feb 15, 2024

    Nirbhik and Carlos present at the Biophysical Society Annual Meeting

    The lab was represented with a poster and a platform talk, and came away with new ideas and collaborations from the meeting.

  • Dec 5, 2023

    Carlos speaks at the Keystone Symposia on Ubiquitin Biology and Disease

    Carlos presented the lab's work on how UBQLN2 affects degradation of ubiquitinated substrates by the proteasome and deubiquitinases, and caught up on the latest developments in the ubiquitin field.

  • Dec 5, 2023

    Anitha and Billy present at ASCB

    Our postdocs represented the lab at the American Society for Cell Biology meeting, showcasing work on how different cellular stresses affect UBQLN2 localization.

  • Dec 5, 2023

    Paper on UBQLN1, UBQLN2, and UBQLN4 phase transitions published in Biophysical Journal

    This study found that the charge states of the short disordered regions in the three ubiquilin paralogs strongly affect their phase transitions — and that even common epitope tags can shift this behavior.

  • Oct 12, 2023

    Paper on polyubiquitin ligand spacing and phase transitions published in PNAS

    In collaboration with Jeremy Schmit (Kansas State University), the lab designed a library of polyubiquitin chains to work out how spacing and architecture along the chain shape UBQLN2 phase transitions.

  • Sep 3, 2023

    Welcomed new lab members: Nirbhik Acharya, Sydney French, and Alana Ramirez

    The lab grew this summer and fall with a new postdoc, a rotation student, and an undergraduate researcher.

  • Jul 14, 2023

    Said goodbye to Sarasi Galagedera and Bridget Varner

    The lab celebrated postdoc Sarasi and lab technician Bridget as they moved on to new roles, with a lab outing despite smoky, wildfire-hazed skies.

  • Jun 8, 2023

    Paper with the Beveridge group published in JACS

    Combining the Beveridge group's ion-mobility mass spectrometry expertise with the lab's work on UBQLN2, this study tracked conformational changes in intrinsically disordered proteins under conditions that promote or reverse phase separation.

  • Oct 6, 2022

    Sarasi and Bridget present at the Gibbs Conference

    At their first in-person Gibbs Conference, Sarasi and Bridget gave a talk and a poster on their work, alongside good company and good weather.
